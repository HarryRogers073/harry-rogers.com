## Project Overview

Hardware schematics, custom PCB power distribution, and bare-metal embedded C firmware for an **Autonomous Combat Robotics Platform** built for the **University of Brighton Robot Wars Tournament** annual Engineering Design competition.

The platform achieved a **2nd Place Podium Finish** in the collegiate combat tournament and earned an **82% Distinction Grade (A+)** for mechanical reliability, control responsiveness, and electrical system design.

---

## Physical Robot Anatomy & 3D CAD

The chassis was engineered to withstand severe physical impacts in the combat arena while maintaining a low centre of gravity for traction and rapid differential turning.


### Mechanical CAD Modelling

Before manufacturing and 3D printing custom brackets, the entire assembly was modelled in CAD to balance mass distribution, gear ratios, and battery placement:

## Circuit Schematics & Electrical Architecture

To prevent motor inductive spikes from resetting the microcontroller during rapid forward-to-reverse transitions, the motor power stage was isolated with flyback diodes, decoupling capacitors, and dedicated ground planes.

![Full Circuit Wiring & Microcontroller Schematic](/images/projects/robot-wars/robot-wiring-schematic.png)
*Figure 2: Complete wiring schematic illustrating PIC microcontroller GPIO routing, motor driver H-bridge interfaces, sonar echo capture, and Hitachi HD44780 LCD pinout.*

## Embedded C Firmware Architecture

The firmware was implemented in bare-metal C using **MPLAB XC8**, structured as a non-blocking finite state machine (FSM) executing real-time sonar range polling, defensive evasion, and aggressive pursuit.

![Embedded C Motor Driver & Steering Routine](/images/projects/robot-wars/code-c-motor-driver.png)
*Figure 3: Core Embedded C differential steering driver mapping tactical states (`DRIVE_FORWARD`, `PIVOT_LEFT`, `DEFENSIVE_SPIN`) to PWM duty cycles and H-bridge direction registers.*
